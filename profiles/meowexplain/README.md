# MeowExplain production profile

Use this profile for English-language MeowExplain Shorts. Apply the shared
[production workflow](../../SKILL.md) for audio, captions, render and packaging.

## Story lock

Start with an action, outcome or contradiction that makes the viewer ask a
specific question. Write the question in the episode sheet and decide which
shot will answer it. On the beat sheet, pair the viewer's current assumption
with a new visible clue, test, or decision. Keep each beat that advances the
answer or raises the stakes. Let the result play long enough to read on a phone.

Use the references by function. SegmentZero shows how an ordinary-looking
condition changes the outcome when the viewer sees it from the right angle.
Ricks Clips makes a character's odd choice clear by revealing motive and
consequences through action. Use whichever approach serves the premise.

### Worked example

*The Skyscraper They Fixed After Dark*

| Beat | What changes | What to show |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | People work inside a building whose walls crews open every night. Why? | Match cut from a daytime desk to the same desk as the night crew removes a panel. |
| Evidence | Wind hitting a corner puts strain on two sides of the tower at once. | Show the wind meeting the corner, then follow the load to the braces and bolts. |
| Stakes | The crew still has joints to reinforce as Hurricane Ella approaches. | Alternate the weld in progress with the storm track. |
| Payoff | Workers kept coming in while crews reinforced the structure behind them. | Return to the desk and reveal the welded plate behind its wall. |

Replace lines like “The engineer ran the numbers again and found a problem”
with a visible discovery. “He checked what happened when the wind hit a corner.
Now two sides were taking the load at once.”

## Character lock

**Use flat 2D head and torso cards with thin stick arms and legs in a 3D set.**
Use the [Skyscraper-derived cast rig](../../reference/src/compositions/meowexplain/cast.ts)
for this profile. Match the line weight, proportions, clothes and facial features
to the approved cast for the episode. Keep the jaw silhouette clear of the
shoulders and collar. Use one pose source per actor across shots and close-ups.

[Open the model sheet](model-sheet.jpg) to compare the silhouettes and contact
poses from different camera angles.

Before full production, review the full body and a close-up from front, ¾ and a
side camera. Then review a short motion clip of sitting, stepping and gripping a
prop. Check the following in the actual edit:

- The head and torso read as flat shapes and limbs keep their thin ink outline.
- Arms and legs stay attached through the cut and throughout the action.
- The hand and prop share the same grip point. Planted feet stay on the floor.
- Desks, panels and rails occlude the correct parts of the character as the
  camera moves.

Check the frame before, during and after each grab or release. Record the rig
revision and reviewed frame ranges in the [episode sheet](../../workflow/episode-sheet.md).
