# Narration for English-language Shorts

Pick a premise the viewer can see and state in one sentence. Decide what the
viewer should know or feel at the last image. For a roughly minute-long Short,
budget the draft against a read in the intended voice. Adjust the copy to fit
breaths, pivotal lines, and time to see the ending.

| Section | What to write | How to check it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pertanyaan awal | Show an unusual event or contradiction on a specific object, person, or action. | After the first image and line, can a listener describe the mystery in one sentence? |
| Yang bikin penonton bertahan | Introduce the character's immediate goal. Demonstrate the rule with an example, then use it to uncover stronger evidence, create a risk, or force a decision. | Does each beat change what the viewer expects? Can the animator show the evidence? |
| Payoff | Answer the opening question with a visible result, then show what it costs or changes. Plant the evidence early if the result reinterprets it. | Can a listener name what happened, why it happened, and how the opening prepared it? |

## Review the beat sheet before writing VO

- Record what the viewer can reasonably assume after the opening shot. At each
  beat, name the new evidence, the action that shows it, and what changes for the
  viewer. Merge beats that repeat the same fact.
- Give the middle a new constraint, choice, or consequence. For a mechanism
  story, change a condition and show its effect. For a character story, reveal
  a detail that changes how the viewer understands an earlier action.
- Describe the last shot in one sentence. Check that it visibly answers the
  opening question and shows the outcome. Leave screen time to read the result.

For MeowExplain, use the [Skyscraper example](../profiles/meowexplain/README.md#worked-example)
when a draft reads like a summary of events.

## Patterns from the two reference Shorts

| Reference | How it sustains attention | What to apply |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *He Waited 40 Years to Open This Door* | The waiting and locks create measurable progress. The characters' guesses make the first reveal meaningful. A later explanation gives the apparently worthless object a different purpose. | Give the viewer clues and a plausible prediction. Let the outcome explain a concrete earlier choice. |
| *Why Every Train Carries Sand* | Each test makes traction worse. A familiar analogy makes the rule understandable before a lever and visible sand demonstrate it. The closing callback carries the explanation into an everyday image. | Escalate the same problem with changed conditions. Show the mechanism and result together. |

Both samples make progress audible through moving beds and short accents.
Consider thinning the bed around the key line, then letting the next action
bring the sound back. Set delivery speed by intelligibility and available
screen time.

## Choose a voice by premise

- **Horror:** Use concrete first or close third person. Keep delivery contained at
  the start, turn on the moment the character understands the danger, and leave
  a beat for the consequence. Keep speaker, place, and cause clear when identity
  shifts.
- **Explainer:** Lead with a surprising result or practical problem, then show the
  mechanism through progressively stronger examples. Use a concrete analogy if a
  viewer needs it to understand the mechanism.
- **Profession / how it feels:** Second-person present works well. Use a visible
  task, a constraint that makes the easy fix fail, and a physical demonstration of
  the eventual solution. See the [mount maker](../examples/museum-mount-maker-narration.md)
  and [foley artist](../examples/foley-artist-narration.md) drafts.

Write a clean spoken script and keep performance notes separately by line or beat.
Convert direction into the syntax of the chosen TTS tool only at recording time.
Listen for pace, pronunciation, dropped or repeated words, and whether the
payoff is understandable on a first listen.

## Worked outline — Eight Seconds Ahead

| Section | Story beat and what the viewer sees |
| --- | --- |
| Pertanyaan awal | On the security monitor, the guard crawls under his desk. In the room, he is still sitting in his chair. Why is he hiding eight seconds from now? |
| Yang bikin penonton bertahan | A door swings on the monitor eight seconds before the real door moves. Outside, the relief guard parks and checks in by radio. Beneath the desk, the guard watches his future thumb hit transmit to warn her. A red LED lights up his hiding place. He presses anyway as she reaches the entrance. |
| Payoff | The intruder at the door sees the flash on the monitor and reaches under the desk as the real radio lights up. The guard gets halfway through the warning before the intruder takes the radio and finishes in the guard's exact voice. The relief guard turns her car around. The monitor shows the impostor, now with the guard's face, settling into his chair. Eight seconds later, the chair creaks in the room. |

Give the doorway a clear sightline to the monitor. Introduce the guard's voice
on the radio before the anomaly and show the relief guard's acknowledgment so
the hand-off and consequence read immediately. Give the monitor, LED and hand
readable screen space. Make the warning worth the risk before he presses.
